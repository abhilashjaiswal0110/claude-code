# Marketing Expertise Skill

Domain expertise for enterprise B2B marketing, content strategy, messaging frameworks, and campaign development.

## When to Use This Skill

Automatically invoked when:
- Creating marketing content (blogs, social, campaigns, press releases)
- Developing messaging or positioning
- Designing marketing campaigns or programs
- Analyzing content performance or strategy
- Brand voice and tone discussions

Manually invoke when:
- Reviewing marketing materials for effectiveness
- Planning content calendars or campaigns
- Evaluating messaging architecture
- Ensuring brand consistency

## Core Marketing Principles

### 1. Audience-First Approach
- **Know your buyer** - Persona, pain points, goals, objections
- **Speak their language** - Industry terms, appropriate sophistication level
- **Address their journey stage** - Awareness, consideration, decision
- **Provide value** - Education > selling

### 2. Clear Value Proposition
**Format: [We help] [audience] [achieve benefit] [how we're different]**

Example:
> "We help enterprise IT teams reduce cloud costs by 30% through AI-powered optimization that learns your usage patterns."

### 3. Proof Over Claims
- Use specific numbers and metrics
- Include customer testimonials and case studies
- Reference industry analysts and certifications
- Show, don't tell (demos, screenshots, examples)

## Content Strategy Framework

### AIDA Model (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action)

**Attention** → Hook: Compelling headline, stat, or question
**Interest** → Build: Relevant problem, industry insight
**Desire** → Engage: Solution benefits, social proof
**Action** → Convert: Clear CTA, low-friction next step

### StoryBrand Framework

1. **Character** - Customer (not your company)
2. **Problem** - External, internal, philosophical
3. **Guide** - Your company (empathy + authority)
4. **Plan** - Simple 3-step process
5. **Call to Action** - Direct + transitional
6. **Success** - What victory looks like
7. **Failure** - What's at stake

## B2B vs B2C Marketing

| Aspect | B2B | B2C |
|--------|-----|-----|
| Decision | Rational, ROI-focused | Emotional, personal |
| Buyer | Committee, multiple stakeholders | Individual |
| Cycle | Long (weeks-months) | Short (minutes-days) |
| Content | Educational, technical | Entertaining, inspirational |
| Channels | LinkedIn, email, webinars | Social, ads, influencers |
| Messaging | Value, efficiency, risk reduction | Lifestyle, status, enjoyment |

**For enterprise B2B:**
- Lead with business outcomes, not features
- Executive summaries for C-level, technical details for practitioners
- Case studies with ROI metrics
- Risk mitigation and security emphasis
- Compliance and integration messaging

## Content Type Guidelines

### Blog Posts (800-1500 words)
**Structure:**
1. Hook headline (promise specific value)
2. Introduction (problem statement, preview)
3. Body (3-5 main points with subheadings)
4. Conclusion (summary, next steps)
5. CTA (download, demo, contact)

**Tips:**
- Use H2/H3 headings every 200-300 words
- Include images, charts, or infographics
- Add pull quotes for skimmers
- Optimize for SEO (keywords, meta description)
- Link to related content

### Social Media

**LinkedIn (B2B focus):**
- Length: 1-300 characters optimal (longer possible)
- Tone: Professional but conversational
- Format: Hook → Insight → CTA
- Hashtags: 3-5 relevant hashtags
- Media: Professional graphics, data viz, videos
- Best practices: Ask questions, share insights, tag relevant people

**Twitter/X:**
- Length: Concise, 1-2 sentences
- Tone: Punchy, direct
- Format: Stat/insight + link
- Hashtags: 1-2 max
- Threads: Break complex topics into 5-8 tweets

### Press Releases
**Structure:**
1. Headline (newsworthy, active voice)
2. Dateline (City, State, Date)
3. Lead paragraph (who, what, when, where, why)
4. Quote from executive
5. Body (details, context, benefits)
6. Quote from customer/partner (if applicable)
7. About section
8. Contact information

**Newsworthy events:**
- Product launches
- Major partnerships
- Funding announcements
- Awards and certifications
- Research/survey results
- Executive appointments

### Email Newsletters
- Subject line: 40-50 characters, avoid spam triggers
- Preheader: Complement subject line
- Above the fold: Most important content/CTA
- Sections: 3-5 distinct sections
- CTAs: 1 primary, 2-3 secondary
- Footer: Unsubscribe, legal, social links

## SEO Optimization

### On-Page SEO Checklist
- [ ] Target keyword in title (H1)
- [ ] Keyword in first 100 words
- [ ] Keyword in at least one H2
- [ ] 3-5 keyword mentions (natural, not stuffed)
- [ ] 2-3 internal links to related content
- [ ] 1-2 authoritative external links
- [ ] Alt text for all images (include keyword if relevant)
- [ ] Meta description (155 characters, keyword included)
- [ ] URL slug (short, keyword-rich)

### Readability
- Sentences: 15-20 words average
- Paragraphs: 2-4 sentences
- Grade level: 8th grade for general, 12th for technical
- Active voice: 80%+ of sentences
- Transition words: 20%+ of sentences

## Brand Voice Development

### Voice Attributes (Pick 3-4)
- Professional / Friendly / Authoritative / Conversational
- Innovative / Reliable / Bold / Understated
- Technical / Accessible / Academic / Practical

### Tone Spectrum
**Formal ←→ Casual**
**Respectful ←→ Irreverent**
**Enthusiastic ←→ Matter-of-fact**
**Serious ←→ Humorous**

**Enterprise B2B typically:**
- Professional but not stuffy
- Confident but not arrogant
- Knowledgeable but not condescending
- Friendly but not overly casual

### Voice Examples

**Technical Feature → Customer Benefit**
❌ "Advanced predictive algorithms with ML-powered optimization"
✅ "Automatically identifies cost savings before they impact your budget"

**Generic → Specific**
❌ "Industry-leading performance"
✅ "Processes 1M transactions per second with 99.99% uptime"

**Company-centric → Customer-centric**
❌ "We've built the most comprehensive platform"
✅ "You get every tool you need in one place"

## Campaign Development

### Campaign Planning Framework
1. **Objective** - What are we trying to achieve? (measurable)
2. **Audience** - Who are we reaching? (specific persona)
3. **Insight** - What do they care about? (research-backed)
4. **Message** - What should they remember? (single idea)
5. **Channels** - Where will we reach them? (media mix)
6. **Assets** - What do we need to create? (content plan)
7. **Timeline** - When does it run? (schedule)
8. **Budget** - How much can we spend? (allocation)
9. **Metrics** - How do we measure success? (KPIs)

### Multi-Touch Campaign Example: Product Launch

**Week 1: Awareness**
- LinkedIn announcement post
- Blog: "Introducing [Product]"
- Email to existing customers

**Week 2-3: Education**
- Webinar: "How [Product] works"
- Blog: Use case deep dive
- Social: Feature highlights (5 posts)

**Week 4-5: Conversion**
- Case study published
- Email campaign: Free trial offer
- Retargeting ads

**Week 6+: Nurture**
- Comparison guide vs competitors
- ROI calculator tool
- Customer success stories

## Compliance and Legal Considerations

### Claims to Avoid (unless provable)
- "Best in class" / "Industry-leading" (subjective)
- "Guaranteed results" (too strong)
- Specific statistics without source
- Competitor disparagement
- Forward-looking statements without disclaimers

### Required Disclaimers
- Pricing: "Starting at..." or "Subject to change"
- Performance: "Results may vary"
- Partnerships: Trademark symbols (™®)
- Financial projections: SEC-compliant language
- Medical/legal: "Consult a professional"

### Content Approval Chain
1. **Marketing** - Message, voice, quality
2. **Product/Engineering** - Technical accuracy
3. **Legal** - Claims, disclaimers, compliance
4. **Executive** - Strategic alignment (for major announcements)

## Performance Metrics

### Top of Funnel (Awareness)
- Impressions
- Reach
- Website traffic
- Social engagement (likes, shares, comments)
- Brand search volume

### Middle of Funnel (Consideration)
- Content downloads
- Webinar registrations
- Email open/click rates
- Time on site
- Pages per session

### Bottom of Funnel (Conversion)
- MQLs (Marketing Qualified Leads)
- SQLs (Sales Qualified Leads)
- Demo requests
- Trial sign-ups
- Closed-won revenue

## Red Flags to Avoid

- ❌ **Hype and buzzwords** - "Revolutionary", "game-changing", "disruptive"
- ❌ **Vague benefits** - "Improve efficiency" (by how much?)
- ❌ **Feature dumps** - List of capabilities without context
- ❌ **No CTA** - What should reader do next?
- ❌ **Generic stock photos** - Use real people, real products
- ❌ **Wall of text** - Break up with visuals, headings, bullets
- ❌ **Talking about yourself** - "We are...", "Our platform..."

## Teaching Moment for Users

**Why marketing is strategic, not just creative:**

Good marketing isn't about creating pretty content - it's about understanding:
1. **Psychology** - What motivates decision-makers?
2. **Economics** - What's the ROI of acquiring this customer?
3. **Data** - What's actually working vs what we think works?
4. **Systems** - How do touchpoints work together?

**Content is not a campaign:**
One blog post isn't a campaign. One email isn't a campaign. A campaign is a coordinated series of touchpoints that move a prospect through a journey.

**B2B marketing is education:**
In B2B, you're not selling widgets - you're helping companies make complex, high-stakes decisions. Your content should make the buyer smarter, not just promote your product.

The best marketing provides value even if the prospect never buys.
