# Recruitment Expertise Skill

Domain expertise for inclusive hiring, bias-free job descriptions, structured interviews, and skills-based candidate evaluation.

## When to Use This Skill

Automatically invoked when:
- Creating job descriptions or postings
- Developing screening criteria or interview questions
- Evaluating or comparing candidates
- Designing hiring processes or rubrics
- Discussing DEI in recruitment

Manually invoke when:
- Auditing job descriptions for bias
- Training hiring managers
- Building interview guides
- Creating offer letters or employment contracts

## Core Recruitment Principles

### 1. Skills-Based Hiring
**Focus on what someone can do, not credentials or years**

❌ "5+ years experience required"
✅ "Demonstrated ability to architect distributed systems (provide portfolio)"

**Why:** Years-of-experience requirements:
- Filter out career changers and non-traditional backgrounds
- Act as age proxies
- Don't correlate with performance
- Reduce diversity in candidate pool

### 2. Inclusive Language
**Write for the candidate you want, not the one you imagine**

Every biased word in a JD reduces applications from underrepresented groups:
- 1-2 biased words: 0-10% drop
- 5+ biased words: 40%+ drop

### 3. Structured Evaluation
**Same questions, same rubric, different candidates**

Unstructured interviews are:
- Only 14% predictive of performance
- Subject to unconscious bias
- Legally risky (inconsistent evaluation)

Structured interviews are:
- 26% predictive of performance
- More equitable across demographic groups
- Legally defensible (consistent process)

## Job Description Framework

### Inclusive JD Structure

```markdown
## About the Role
[Mission: Why this role exists, impact on company/team]

## What You'll Do
[Responsibilities: Specific tasks and projects, not vague duties]

## What We're Looking For

### Essential Skills (Must-Have)
- [Specific skill with measurable criteria]
- [Max 5-6 items]

### Desired Skills (Nice-to-Have)
- [Skills that would help but aren't required]
- [Signal that partial matches should apply]

### Working Style
- [Team dynamics, collaboration style, work environment]

## What We Offer
[Compensation, benefits, growth, culture]
[Include salary range if legally required or company policy]

## Our Commitment to Inclusion
[DEI statement, accommodation notice, EEO language]

## How to Apply
[Clear instructions, timeline, what to submit]
```

### Bias Detection Guide

| Biased Language | Why It's Problematic | Better Alternative |
|----------------|---------------------|-------------------|
| "Recent graduate" | Age discrimination | "0-2 years experience" or just list skills |
| "Digital native" | Age proxy | "Comfortable with modern tools" |
| "Culture fit" | Masks discrimination | "Values alignment: [list specific values]" |
| "Native English speaker" | National origin bias | "English proficiency required" |
| "Energetic", "dynamic" | Age/disability proxies | "Motivated", "proactive" |
| "Strong/healthy/athletic" | Disability discrimination | Only if genuinely job-related with specifics |
| "Rock star", "ninja", "guru" | Gendered, exclusionary | "Skilled", "expert", "proficient" |
| "Aggressive" sales | Gendered | "Proactive", "results-driven" |
| "He/him" pronouns | Gendered | "They/them" or remove pronouns |
| "Fast-paced startup" | Age/disability proxy | "Growth-stage company" |

### Skills vs Years Translation

| Years-based | Skills-based |
|-------------|-------------|
| "5+ years JavaScript" | "Can architect scalable SPAs with React/Vue (portfolio required)" |
| "3+ years management" | "Experience leading teams of 3-5, coaching, and giving feedback" |
| "10+ years engineering" | "Senior-level systems design, mentorship, and technical leadership" |

### Reducing Barriers

**Don't require:**
- Specific degrees if skills can be demonstrated otherwise
- Relocation if role can be remote
- Availability outside business hours unless truly necessary
- Specific tools if transferable skills exist
- Cover letters (they favor privileged candidates with time/resources)

**Do include:**
- Clear compensation range (increases apply rate, reduces negotiation gaps)
- Remote/hybrid options if feasible
- Accommodation notice (ADA requirement)
- Application time estimate ("< 15 minutes")
- Alternative qualification paths ("OR equivalent demonstrated skills")

## Structured Interview Design

### Interview Types and Purposes

| Type | Purpose | Example |
|------|---------|---------|
| **Behavioral** | Past performance predicts future | "Tell me about a time you had to resolve a conflict..." |
| **Situational** | Problem-solving approach | "How would you handle a situation where..." |
| **Technical** | Skills assessment | Coding challenge, case study, portfolio review |
| **Values** | Culture and mission alignment | "What kind of work environment brings out your best?" |
| **Reverse** | Candidate evaluates us | "What questions do you have for us?" |

### Creating Interview Questions

**Bad question characteristics:**
- Vague ("Tell me about yourself")
- Hypothetical without context ("What's your greatest weakness?")
- Trivia ("What happens when you type a URL?")
- Leading ("You like working hard, right?")
- Illegal (age, marital status, religion, etc.)

**Good question characteristics:**
- Specific to job requirements
- Open-ended (not yes/no)
- Behavioral with STAR format prompt (Situation, Task, Action, Result)
- Equal difficulty for all candidates
- Clear evaluation criteria defined in advance

### Example Interview Guide: Senior Software Engineer

**Question 1: Technical Problem-Solving (30 min)**
> "Walk me through how you would design a URL shortener service that needs to handle 1000 requests per second."

**Rubric:**
- 4 = Comprehensive system design with trade-offs, scalability, and edge cases
- 3 = Solid design with some scalability considerations
- 2 = Basic design, missing key components
- 1 = Incomplete or fundamentally flawed design

**Question 2: Collaboration (15 min)**
> "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a technical decision made by your team. What was it, and how did you handle it?"

**Rubric:**
- 4 = Constructive disagreement, data-driven, influenced outcome, team cohesion maintained
- 3 = Raised concerns appropriately, participated in resolution
- 2 = Disagreed but didn't engage constructively
- 1 = Avoided conflict or handled unprofessionally

**Question 3: Mentorship (10 min)**
> "Describe a time you helped a junior engineer grow their skills. What was your approach?"

**Rubric:**
- 4 = Proactive mentorship, tailored approach, measurable growth
- 3 = Provided guidance when asked, helpful
- 2 = Minimal mentorship, primarily directive
- 1 = No mentorship experience or interest

## Candidate Evaluation Framework

### Evaluation Dimensions

**For every role, define:**
1. **Must-have skills** (disqualifies if missing)
2. **Core competencies** (weighted by importance)
3. **Growth potential** (learning agility, adaptability)
4. **Team fit** (not culture fit - specific team needs)
5. **Values alignment** (specific to company values)

### Scoring System

**Use consistent scale:**
- 4 = Exceeds expectations for role
- 3 = Meets expectations (good hire)
- 2 = Meets some expectations (marginal)
- 1 = Does not meet expectations

**Aggregate:** Weight by importance, sum scores

**Decision threshold:**
- 3.5+ = Strong hire
- 3.0-3.4 = Hire
- 2.5-2.9 = Maybe (tie-breaker round)
- <2.5 = Pass

### Candidate Comparison Best Practices

**Do:**
- Use the same rubric for all candidates
- Record scores before discussing
- Document specific evidence for scores
- Focus on job-relevant criteria
- Consider team gaps (skills needed)
- Weight recent performance more than distant past

**Don't:**
- Compare to "ideal" candidate (compare to role requirements)
- Let one interviewer dominate discussion
- Anchor on first impressions
- Use "gut feel" without specific reasoning
- Consider protected characteristics
- Compare based on "culture fit" without definition

### Documenting Hiring Decisions

**For legal compliance, document:**
- Clear job requirements before posting
- Consistent interview questions per role
- Scoring rubric used
- Individual scores and aggregates
- Specific behavioral evidence for decisions
- Business justification for selection

**Never document:**
- Protected class information (unless related to affirmative action)
- Subjective "gut feelings"
- Non-job-related preferences
- Comparative statements about protected groups

## Offer Letter Components

### Standard Elements
1. **Position title**
2. **Start date**
3. **Reporting structure**
4. **Compensation**
   - Base salary
   - Bonus structure (if applicable)
   - Equity (if applicable)
   - Payment frequency
5. **Benefits summary** (with reference to full plan docs)
6. **Work arrangement** (remote/hybrid/onsite, location)
7. **At-will employment disclaimer** (if applicable)
8. **Contingencies**
   - Background check
   - Reference checks
   - Proof of authorization to work
   - Drug screening (if applicable)
9. **Confidentiality and IP assignment** (reference to agreements)
10. **Expiration date** for offer acceptance

### Offer Negotiation

**Negotiable typically:**
- Base salary (within budget range)
- Start date
- Sign-on bonus (to offset lost unvested equity from prior employer)
- Equity (if role has equity bands)
- Title (if responsibilities align)

**Usually not negotiable:**
- Benefits (standardized across company)
- Work hours/schedule (unless role-specific)
- Performance metrics
- PTO policy (company-wide)

**Red flags in negotiation:**
- Unrealistic expectations far outside market
- Unwilling to compromise at all
- Sharing competitor offers without context
- Negotiating items already agreed upon

## Diversity and Inclusion Best Practices

### Sourcing for Diversity
- Post on diverse job boards (eg. PowerToFly, Jopwell, Fairygodboss)
- Partner with HBCUs, women-in-tech orgs, veteran groups
- Use inclusive language checkers (eg. Textio,Gender Decoder)
- Remove degree requirements where not essential
- Offer apprenticeships or fellowship programs

### Reducing Bias in Screening
- **Blind resume review** - Remove names, universities, graduation years
- **Skills tests before resume review** - Use work samples to filter
- **Structured phone screens** - Same questions for all candidates
- **Diverse hiring committees** - Multiple perspectives reduce individual bias

### Interview Panel Composition
- Include interviewers from different backgrounds
- At least one person from underrepresented group (if possible)
- Mix of levels (peer, manager, skip-level)
- Avoid all-male panels for any candidate

### Accommodation Best Practices
- Proactively mention accommodation availability in all communications
- Respond quickly to accommodation requests
- Offer alternatives (eg. phone screen vs video for accessibility)
- Extend deadlines for assessments if requested
- Don't ask follow-up questions about why accommodation is needed

## Red Flags: Legal Risks

**Never ask about:**
- Age, birthdate, or graduation year (age discrimination)
- Marital status, children, family plans (gender discrimination)
- National origin, citizenship, or native language (national origin discrimination)
- Disability, health, or medical conditions (disability discrimination)
- Religious affiliation or practices (religious discrimination)
- Arrest record (some jurisdictions prohibit)
- Salary history (illegal in many jurisdictions)
- Genetic information (GINA violation)

**Exception:** Can ask about authorization to work in country (but not citizenship)

## Teaching Moment for Users

**Why structured hiring matters:**

Unstructured hiring is basically "Who do I like?" which:
- Favors candidates similar to interviewers
- Misses diverse talent
- Has low predictive validity (bad hires)
- Exposes company to discrimination claims

Structured hiring is "Who can do the job best?" which:
- Focuses on skills and performance
- Increases diversity
- Predicts success more accurately
- Provides legal defensibility

**The diversity business case:**

Diverse teams:
- Are 35-45% more likely to outperform non-diverse teams (McKinsey)
- Produce better products (wider perspective = fewer blind spots)
- Improve decision-making (challenges groupthink)
- Reflect customer base more accurately

But diversity only works with inclusion - hiring diverse people into a homogenous culture doesn't work.

**Skills vs credentials:**

The degree requirement has been one of the strongest barriers to opportunity:
- Only 37% of Americans have bachelor's degree
- Many high-performers are self-taught or bootcamp-trained
- Degree correlates with privilege, not always with skill

Skills-based hiring expands talent pool without compromising quality.
