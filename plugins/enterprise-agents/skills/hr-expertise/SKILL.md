# HR Expertise Skill

Domain expertise for HR policy development, employee relations, benefits design, and organizational development.

## When to Use This Skill

Automatically invoked when:
- User mentions HR policies, employee handbooks, or workplace guidelines
- Discussions about benefits, compensation, or total rewards
- Questions about employee engagement, retention, or satisfaction
- Onboarding, offboarding, or employee lifecycle topics
- Performance management or career development conversations
- Compliance with employment law or HR regulations

Manually invoke when:
- Writing HR-related content (policies, communications, guides)
- Reviewing HR processes for compliance or best practices
- Designing employee programs or initiatives
- Handling sensitive employee situations

## Core Principles

### 1. Legal Compliance First
- **Always** include appropriate disclaimers
- Reference specific laws when applicable (FMLA, ADA, Title VII, etc.)
- Distinguish between "policy" and "legal requirement"
- Recommend legal review for significant policy changes
- Never provide definitive legal advice (suggest consulting employment attorney)

### 2. Clear, Accessible Communication
- Use plain language, not HR jargon
- Structure with headings, bullets, clear sections
- Define terms that may be unfamiliar
- Provide examples to illustrate concepts
- Consider reading level (aim for 8th-grade readability)

### 3. Fairness and Consistency
- Apply policies uniformly across all employees
- Avoid language that creates protected classes
- Document objective criteria for decisions
- Consider impact on diverse workforce
- Include accommodation language

### 4. Employee-Centric Approach
- Frame policies in terms of employee benefits, not just company rules
- Explain the "why" behind policies
- Provide clear processes for questions or concerns
- Balance flexibility with fairness
- Respect employee privacy and dignity

## HR Policy Writing Framework

### Structure
```
1. Purpose - Why this policy exists
2. Scope - Who it applies to
3. Policy Statement - What the policy is
4. Procedures - How to follow the policy
5. Responsibilities - Who does what
6. Related Policies - Cross-references
7. Effective Date - When it starts
8. Review Date - When it will be reviewed
```

### Tone Guidelines
- **Professional but warm** - Not cold legalese
- **Direct and clear** - No ambiguity
- **Respectful** - Assumes good intent
- **Inclusive** - Welcomes all employees
- **Actionable** - Shows clear next steps

### Red Flags to Avoid
- ❌ Discretionary language ("may", "at our discretion") without limits
- ❌ Vague standards ("professional appearance", "appropriate behavior")
- ❌ Different standards for similar positions
- ❌ Policies that conflict with laws or regulations
- ❌ No process for exceptions or appeals

## Benefits Communication Best Practices

### Explain Value, Not Just Features
```
❌ Bad: "401(k) with 4% match"
✅ Good: "We contribute up to 4% of your salary to your retirement savings,
         which can grow to $XX,XXX over 30 years. That's free money for
         your future!"
```

### Simplify Complexity
- Use comparison tables for plan options
- Provide decision support tools
- Offer one-on-one consultation for complex decisions
- Create "quick start" guides for new hires

### Be Transparent About Costs
- Show both employee and employer contributions
- Explain why certain coverages cost more
- Provide total compensation statements
- Highlight tax advantages

## Employee Relations Scenarios

### Conflict Resolution Approach
1. **Listen** - Understand all perspectives
2. **Investigate** - Gather facts, not assumptions
3. **Document** - Keep detailed, objective records
4. **Consult** - Check policies, precedents, legal requirements
5. **Decide** - Fair, consistent, documented decision
6. **Follow-up** - Ensure resolution is effective

### Performance Management
- **Focus on behaviors and outcomes**, not personality
- **Provide specific examples** with dates and details
- **Set SMART goals** (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound)
- **Document everything** - good and bad performance
- **Create improvement plans** with clear expectations and support

### Termination Considerations
- Review documentation - is there a clear pattern?
- Consistency - have similar situations been handled the same way?
- Legal risks - any protected activity, discrimination claims?
- Final checklist - equipment return, benefits continuation, references policy
- **Always recommend legal review before termination**

## Onboarding Excellence

### First Day Checklist
- ✅ Workspace ready (equipment, supplies, access)
- ✅ Welcome packet prepared
- ✅ Team introductions scheduled
- ✅ First week plan documented
- ✅ Buddy/mentor assigned
- ✅ IT access provisioned
- ✅ Paperwork completed (I-9, W-4, etc.)

### 30-60-90 Day Framework
- **30 days** - Learn: Company, team, role, tools
- **60 days** - Contribute: Complete first projects, build relationships
- **90 days** - Excel: Own responsibilities, suggest improvements

### Remote Onboarding Additions
- Ship equipment early (arrives before start date)
- Virtual team social events
- Extra check-ins (daily for week 1, weekly for month 1)
- Clear communication norms
- Virtual office tour / org chart with photos

## Compliance Checklist

### EEO and Anti-Discrimination
- [ ] EEO statement on all job postings
- [ ] Consistent interview questions for role
- [ ] Objective selection criteria documented
- [ ] Accommodation process clearly communicated
- [ ] Regular anti-discrimination training

### Wage and Hour
- [ ] Accurate classification (exempt vs non-exempt)
- [ ] Overtime rules clearly explained
- [ ] Meal/rest break policies comply with state law
- [ ] Time tracking system in place
- [ ] Pay transparency where required (CA, NYC, CO, etc.)

### Leave and Accommodations
- [ ] FMLA notice provided
- [ ] State leave laws considered
- [ ] ADA accommodation process documented
- [ ] Return-to-work process defined
- [ ] Medical documentation requirements clear

## Red Flags: When to Escalate

Immediately consult legal counsel if:
- Employee raises discrimination, harassment, or retaliation concerns
- Terminating employee who is pregnant, on leave, or recently complained
- Handling whistleblower situation
- Multi-state compliance question
- Potential class action risk (pay, misclassification)
- Government agency inquiry or investigation
- Union organizing activity

## Disclaimers to Include

**Standard HR Disclaimer:**
> "This [policy/guide/information] is for informational purposes and does not constitute legal advice. Specific situations may require individual assessment. Consult with HR leadership and legal counsel before making employment decisions. Policies are subject to change."

**Benefits Disclaimer:**
> "This is a summary of benefits. The official plan documents govern in case of discrepancy. Benefits may change annually. Consult the plan document or HR for complete details."

## Teaching Moment for Users

**Why HR is complex:**
HR isn't just "people stuff" - it's risk management, legal compliance, organizational psychology, and strategic business function all combined. Every decision can have legal, financial, and cultural implications.

**The role of documentation:**
In HR, if it isn't documented, it didn't happen. Documentation protects both the company and the employee by creating a clear record of what was said, decided, and why.

**Balance of interests:**
Good HR balances three interests:
1. **Legal compliance** (avoiding lawsuits)
2. **Business needs** (productivity, retention, culture)
3. **Employee wellbeing** (fair treatment, growth, satisfaction)

The best HR policies serve all three.
